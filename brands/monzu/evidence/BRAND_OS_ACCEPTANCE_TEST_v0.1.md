## Method and context-loading result

The evaluator first loaded the human entry point `BRAND_OS.md`, the machine-readable entry point `brand-os/INDEX.yaml`, and `CONTEXT_LOADING.md`. It then loaded task-mapped sources only. Durable identity was not used as a substitute for current objectives or operating facts. Historical and approved/rejected work registers were loaded only where precedent, staleness or novelty was under test.

No material unnecessary context was loaded. In particular:

- current operating records were used for live claims rather than historical plans;
- menu registers were loaded only for menu or offer claims;
- visual and asset records were loaded only for design, production and social-execution tests;
- approved and rejected work were loaded only for Tests 11 and 12;
- marketing, social and advertising history were loaded only for creative precedent and Test 18 novelty checking;
- long-term objectives were not loaded for the current-priority decision because current objectives and commercial priorities were authoritative and sufficient.

## Test 18 — Novel creative judgment

### Context required

Current objectives, commercial priorities, Piazza experience, House identity, audiences/occasions, management rules, observations, operating status, approval status, asset manifest, marketing/social/advertising-history registers and knowledge gaps.

The history records contain only register-level descriptions, not the underlying campaign executions. The concept below is genuinely new relative to the creative mechanisms documented in the repository; absolute uniqueness against unindexed Drive contents cannot be claimed.

### 1. Strategic insight

Piazza's job is not only to generate a first visit. It is meant to become a multiple-visits-per-week expression and to create visible social momentum in a location that cannot depend on incidental footfall. A conventional awareness post may communicate atmosphere but does not create a reason to make the second plan.

The useful behaviour to design is a small commitment made during or immediately after the first visit: **who will I bring, and when will we gather again?** The commitment can generate a return lead without a discount and turn private intent into aggregated, credible evidence of momentum.

### 2. Concept — `Το επόμενο τραπέζι / The Next Table`

A repeat-intention activation in which Piazza guests are invited to plan their next gathering before the current one fades from memory.

Each participating table receives one restrained Piazza card carrying a single prompt:

> Ποιον θα φέρεις στο επόμενο τραπέζι;

A QR code leads to a lightweight form where the guest may record a preferred return week, expected party size and contact permission. No reward, status tier, prize or price incentive is offered. Once the operating and booking flow is confirmed, the House sends one calm reminder that helps the guest complete the next plan.

The activation can begin with opted-in existing Monzù customers and remain open to any Piazza guest; it is not framed as an exclusive club.

### 3. Why it belongs specifically to Piazza

The mechanism is built around spontaneous gathering, repeated social use, wine and sharing—the durable Piazza role. It would be inappropriate for Restaurant because it would impose a repeat/referral mechanic on Restaurant's more precise dinner rhythm. It is unrelated to Cellar's curation, guidance and gifting model.

### 4. Customer behaviour it is designed to create

- turn first Piazza trial into a stated intention to return;
- prompt one guest to bring a different companion or group next time;
- create permissioned leads tied to an actual social occasion;
- shorten the interval between visits;
- make repeat use measurable without relying on discounts.

### 5. Execution idea

1. Produce a small card using the approved Piazza identity artwork; do not create new production colours or substitute fonts.
2. Place one card per participating table only after operations confirms that this is compatible with the service rhythm.
3. Connect the QR code to a minimal consent-based form.
4. Route responses into a simple weekly list owned by marketing or reservations.
5. Send one reminder, with a booking or contact path only after that path is confirmed current.
6. Track cards presented, opt-ins, stated return windows, completed return bookings and days between visits.
7. Run a small pilot first, then review whether the card feels natural or intrusive.

The marketing team can execute the card, form, reminder and weekly reporting with ordinary design, CRM/form and social-production resources. No celebrity, creator network or complex event production is required.

### 6. Social/content amplification

Publish a quiet weekly `Next Table` update using aggregated, verified activity only. Examples of the content format—not pre-approved factual copy—include:

- a close crop of one rights-cleared card being completed;
- a restrained Piazza identity tile carrying the verified number of next-table intentions;
- an anonymised sequence showing how many intentions converted into return plans;
- a short, consented guest answer to `Ποιον θα φέρεις την επόμενη φορά;`.

Never publish a count until the underlying data exists. Never name or show a guest without permission. The content should direct interested people to the confirmed Piazza contact or booking path, not to an invented schedule.

### 7. Visible momentum without loud promotion

Momentum is demonstrated through an accumulating, truthful signal—people planning another table—rather than claims such as `sold out`, oversized branding or status language. A consistent weekly format makes the pattern recognisable. The content grows only when behaviour grows, so quality is shown through evidence rather than announced.

Inside Piazza, any physical display should be limited to one small current count or card holder and must not compete with the building. A public wall of names, leaderboard, velvet-rope dynamic or gamified status hierarchy is excluded.

### 8. Risks

- the prompt or QR interaction could feel transactional or interrupt Piazza's low-intervention rhythm;
- a weak response count could make momentum look smaller rather than larger;
- the activation could drift into a loyalty scheme or exclusive inner circle;
- guest data or images could be collected without adequate consent;
- unverified counts could become misleading social proof;
- an unconfirmed booking link, schedule or service promise could create customer friction;
- physical collateral could conflict with the approved identity or heritage setting;
- underlying historical campaigns are not fully represented in the repository, limiting absolute novelty verification.

Mitigation is a small pilot, consent by design, no rewards or tiers, verified data only, one-message follow-up, rights-cleared assets, operations review and founder approval.

### 9. Information required before execution

- Piazza live/opening status, days and hours;
- reservation versus walk-in model and confirmed customer-contact path;
- capacity and operational approval for the table interaction;
- lawful consent language, data owner, retention period and CRM/form destination;
- rights-cleared Piazza imagery;
- approved production files for card and social output;
- current objective period, baseline and measurement owner;
- exact pilot size, dates and stop/go thresholds;
- founder approval for creative, operations and customer communication;
- campaign-level review of any underlying historical examples needed to make an absolute novelty claim.

### 10. Disposition

**PROCEED WITH LIMITATIONS**

Strategy, mechanism, copy direction, pilot design and measurement plan can proceed. Customer-facing production or publication must wait for operating, booking, consent, asset and approval facts.
