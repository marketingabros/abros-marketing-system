# Carousel and Story sequence method

## Choose the sequence type

Use a carousel when ordered, revisit-able content helps compare, explain, reveal, demonstrate or tell an editorial/lifestyle sequence. Use Stories when current state, proximity, reminder, lightweight participation, proof-in-motion or time-bounded progression matters. Choose neither if a single asset communicates the job more clearly.

## Carousel

- First slide: establish a specific truthful promise, tension or point of view; branding alone is not a reason to swipe.
- Middle: each slide advances one step, contrast, proof point or narrative beat.
- Final: deliver the promised resolution and only then choose a relevant action.
- Balance copy and visual evidence according to comprehension. Do not turn every slide into a miniature poster.
- Save/share utility is a hypothesis tied to genuine future usefulness or interpersonal relevance, not a mandatory CTA.

## Stories

- Define the sequence job and current state before the first frame.
- Orient quickly, then progress through proof, update, participation, reminder or action.
- Use interaction only when the response informs the audience, brand or next sequence and can be governed safely.
- Vary reminders by new information, proof, urgency or audience need; repeated identical asks become spam.
- Verify time-sensitive facts immediately before approval.

## Diagnosis

Use frame/slide behavior only with current definitions and matched sequences. Locate where continuation changed, then consider promise mismatch, comprehension load, redundant frames, weak proof, premature action, audience mix, distribution and external context. Do not infer a universal ideal sequence length from aggregate benchmarks.
