# Phase 6A — Creative invention failure analysis

Status: diagnostic only. No production Skill, agent instruction, evaluation contract or governed learning is changed by this analysis.

Date: 2026-09-21

## Decision

The creative reasoning pipeline is not breaking at strategy formation or at rejection discipline. It is breaking principally in the middle, between a useful strategic tension and an ownable organising idea.

The recurring pattern is:

1. identify a valid behaviour and tension;
2. generate several named mechanisms or visual structures;
3. converge on familiar human/action patterns;
4. attach brand or product evidence as setting, proof or choreography;
5. discover during critique that the premise survives removal or substitution of the brand/product.

Task 2 reached a strategically coherent campaign mechanic, but the portable premise—one guest invites another and later becomes the inviter—needed actual Piazza details and unusually specific relationship stories to earn distinction. Task 4 made the failure more visible: seven surface mechanisms narrowed to the same category proposition, “compact multi-activity variety,” and the two survivors worked for a generic activity box.

This is therefore a **narrow creative-invention weakness**: good strategic diagnosis and evaluation are not consistently producing a consequential product/brand premise or a non-obvious creative leap. The strongest immediate causes are the current Skill method and prompt contract, compounded by orchestration that asks one reasoning path to generate and self-prune. Task 4 also has a material knowledge constraint. Repeated failure with rich Monzù context makes “missing knowledge” insufficient as the general explanation. A model-capability ceiling is plausible but not isolated by the present evidence.

## Evidence base and limits

### Task 2 — Monzù Piazza launch momentum

- Source task: Codex task `01a09fde-5f73-7130-92bd-d0d7e06248fe`; continuation turn `01a0a66b-82cf-7391-b22d-77b600bd7cee`.
- Saved source artifacts: `work-in-progress/piazza-launch/campaign.md` and `evaluation.md` in the source task workspace, recovered through the task record.
- Five territories were generated: **Η παρέα συνεχίζεται**, **Οι μικρές αφορμές**, **Το σπίτι, σε χρήση**, **Από τη μέση**, and **Έτσι ακούγεται η Piazza**.
- The selected mechanism joined trial, return and peer introduction. The first independent review found familiar opening/invitation devices, weak public narrative stakes and only contextual distinctiveness. It required a specific invitation reason, a real Piazza detail, a companion response, a sharper role-reversal opening and a genuine-return pilot.
- After conceptual revision the verdict was `PASS_WITH_CORRECTIONS` / `YES WITH CORRECTIONS`. Distinctiveness, momentum potential and campaign longevity remained 7/10. The evaluation explicitly said the mechanism remained portable and real guest stories had not yet supplied final distinction.
- The work itself admitted that a café could copy “bring a friend,” that actual House/Piazza details were needed to make it belong to Monzù, and that later stages depended on genuine repeat visits.

### Task 4 — My Treasure Bags creative stress test

- Source artifacts: `work-in-progress/my-treasure-bags-task-4/creative-output.md`, `independent-evaluation.md`, `independent-evaluation.json`, `task-plan.json` and `context-accounting.json`.
- Seven territories were generated through transformation, reverse compression, spatial metaphor, branching, continuity, constraint and chapter interruption.
- Five were rejected in the first cut. **The Table Changes Its Mind** and **Reverse Treasure** survived to execution, subtraction and substitution testing.
- Both survivors remained clear without branding or production polish, but both could be used substantially unchanged by a generic mixed activity box. Product specificity scored `3 — FATAL`; creative leap and originality scored 5/10; social-native quality scored 6/10.
- The evaluator found real mechanism-level range but narrower proposition-level range: most routes returned to portability, variety and in-between use. It required inspection of a current product’s exact contents, interaction logic, curation principle, refill relationship, construction and bag-specific enablement before another invention round.
- The correct final verdict was `NO IDEA YET MEETS THE STANDARD`.

### Interpretation limit

Task 2 supplies rich brand truth but no fully ownable campaign platform. Task 4 supplies a deliberately hard substitution test but lacks a Brand OS and decisive product-owned truth. Together they demonstrate the repeated output pattern; they do not by themselves prove whether the irreducible cause is the base model. A controlled rerun after the smallest method, knowledge and orchestration interventions is required to test that.

## Pipeline analysis

### 1. Strategic tension

**Finding: largely intact; not the primary break.**

**Task 2 evidence.** The work identified a commercially useful movement from “we should arrange to go” to a first Piazza visit and then a return with someone new. It recognised the risk that attractive atmosphere could produce admiration without action. The evaluation scored strategic quality 8/10 and commercial relevance 8/10.

**Task 4 evidence.** The tension “small container, multiple next moves” was clear, restrained and connected to product comprehension. Strategic connection scored 8/10. The problem is that this was a category tension, not yet a My Treasure Bags-owned tension.

**Where the problem lies.** Primarily `SKILL` and `PROMPT CONTRACT`, but only at the boundary between strategic and creative tension. The method asks for “a customer tension” plus “a brand truth,” yet does not require the tension itself to change when the brand/product premise is removed. `KNOWLEDGE` materially constrained Task 4.

**Smallest likely intervention.** Split tension into two required statements before ideation: (a) the category/human tension and (b) the brand/product contradiction. Require a removal test on the second: if deleting the consequential product truth leaves the tension intact, it is only a strategic starting point, not an invention brief.

### 2. Divergence

**Finding: mechanism-level divergence is adequate; premise-level divergence is weak.**

**Task 2 evidence.** The five routes used invitation/relay, ordinary occasions, environment-in-use, sharing gestures and sound. That is genuine formal range. Yet several stayed within the same broad promise of easier social gathering, and the selected route used the familiar “bring someone” pattern.

**Task 4 evidence.** Seven devices looked different on paper, but the evaluator found that most collapsed to portability, variety and in-between use. Several sets of three executions were format variations rather than proof of a broad territory.

**Where the problem lies.** `SKILL`, `PROMPT CONTRACT` and `AGENT ORCHESTRATION`. The current instruction asks for different mechanisms and even supplies a menu of mechanisms. That can diversify presentation while leaving the underlying proposition, human role and consequence unchanged.

**Smallest likely intervention.** Add a pre-critique divergence map with four columns—product/brand premise, human role, action pattern and accumulating consequence. Routes occupying the same cells count as one family. Require regeneration from unused cells before any selection.

### 3. Product / brand specificity

**Finding: primary failure. Brand/product truth is often appended as evidence rather than made causally necessary to the idea.**

**Task 2 evidence.** The House entrance, shared Piazza food and lower-intervention service improved fit, but the evaluation said the invitation mechanism remained portable. Distinction depended on finding a specific reason, a true Piazza detail and a genuine response for every episode. The brand supplied context and story selection rules more than the originating premise.

**Task 4 evidence.** Both survivors expressed “multiple activities fit in a portable container.” A generic activity box could perform the same transformation or reverse-compression structure. The bag/zip improved choreography but did not own the concept. Missing construction, curation, refill and interaction evidence meant the system lacked the raw material for a product-caused idea.

**Where the problem lies.** `SKILL` and `KNOWLEDGE` primarily; `PROMPT CONTRACT` secondarily. The Skill asks for a “brand right-to-play,” which permits relevance or credibility when causal necessity is needed. Task 4 should have distinguished “enough information to explore” from “enough product truth to judge ownability.”

**Smallest likely intervention.** Before territory generation, require a short inventory of consequential truths using the sentence: “Only this brand/product can cause the idea because ___.” If no defensible completion exists, return a targeted evidence request or explicitly limit the round to category communication; do not promise an ownable territory.

### 4. Creative leap

**Finding: the conversion from strategy to idea is too literal and one move short.**

**Task 2 evidence.** Strategy (“make return and introduction visible”) became a direct documentary relay. The revised role reversal improved clarity, but the opening invitation and basic human pattern were familiar. The evaluator’s concern shifted from comprehension to whether actual stories could make the portable mechanism distinctive.

**Task 4 evidence.** Product proposition (“compact variety”) became match-cut transformation or reverse packing. Both are coherent visualisations of the proposition, not a surprising reinterpretation of it. Creative leap scored 5/10.

**Where the problem lies.** `SKILL`, `PROMPT CONTRACT` and possibly `MODEL CAPABILITY`. The method proceeds directly from tension/truth to a named mechanism. It has no mandatory intermediate operation that forces collision, inversion, rule change, impossible consequence, cultural reframing or transfer from a remote domain.

**Smallest likely intervention.** Add one invention pass between truth and territory: generate several transformations of each product-owned truth using mutually exclusive operations, then express each candidate as “truth → unexpected rule → audience consequence.” Reject candidates that merely demonstrate or illustrate the input truth.

### 5. Campaign mechanic

**Finding: the system can create behaviour, but it does not reliably create an accumulating engine that is both self-renewing and under the brand’s control.**

**Task 2 evidence.** The selected route had a real sequence: guest becomes inviter, later introductions connect, and earlier episodes can cause later ones. This is the strongest part of Task 2. However, momentum and longevity remained 7/10 because the campaign depended on a supply of voluntary genuine returns; without them the fallback lost the engine. The campaign may record momentum more than create it.

**Task 4 evidence.** The two survivors were repeatable visual demonstrations, not behavioural systems. Their tension reset each time and accumulated no audience contribution, product state, proof or consequence. Social-native quality was therefore secondary to film craft.

**Where the problem lies.** `SKILL` and `EVAL`. The framework assesses social-native and commercial role, but it does not separately require a campaign state transition, source of the next episode, or consequence that compounds.

**Smallest likely intervention.** Add a campaign-engine check for territory work: input, participant action, visible state change, source of the next episode, accumulation and failure fallback. A repeatable look is not a mechanic; a mechanic must alter what can happen next.

### 6. Social-native quality

**Finding: inconsistent. Social is sometimes an actual behaviour layer and sometimes only a sequence-friendly distribution surface.**

**Task 2 evidence.** Private sending, voluntary replies and connected episodes materially supported the invitation behaviour. Social-native quality scored 8/10. Even here, watching unknown people return was not automatically compelling, so public value depended on unusually specific stories.

**Task 4 evidence.** Anticipation, match cuts, reveal, Stories and muted comprehension fit Instagram consumption, but the ideas were primarily films. Participation, conversation, sharing and network effects were secondary; the score was 6/10.

**Where the problem lies.** `SKILL` and `PROMPT CONTRACT`. The present definition groups participation, sequence, immediacy, sharing, conversation and feed context, allowing passive sequential craft to pass the same criterion as a genuinely social mechanism.

**Smallest likely intervention.** Separate `platform-native expression` from `social consequence`. Require an explicit answer to: “What can happen here because people can respond, send, remix, contribute, witness change over time or affect the next instalment?” Mark a route as film-native, not social-native, when the answer is only motion or sequence.

### 7. Ownability

**Finding: tested too late and too softly.**

**Task 2 evidence.** The campaign passed concept selection before the evaluator made portability explicit. Monzù details were then used to repair distinction. The final evaluation still said the mechanism remained portable and did not claim unique ownership.

**Task 4 evidence.** The substitution test correctly killed both developed survivors, but only after seven territories, a five-route cut and full execution development. Earlier territory descriptions already admitted generic activity-box substitution.

**Where the problem lies.** `SKILL`, `PROMPT CONTRACT` and `EVAL`. “Could another brand run it unchanged?” is present, but “unchanged” is too permissive: cosmetic substitutions can disguise an unchanged premise. The decision rules treat brand right as strong enough without demanding causal dependency.

**Smallest likely intervention.** Move two gates before survivor selection: premise removal and nearest-substitute replacement. Ask what logically breaks—not what visual details change—when the brand/product is removed. A high-level territory cannot survive if only props, copy, setting or logo require replacement.

### 8. Idea vs execution

**Finding: production devices repeatedly make category propositions feel more original than they are.**

**Task 2 evidence.** Actual entrance matching, changed camera perspective, real chronology and documentary voices improved the work, but these are executional devices around a portable invitation/role-reversal premise. The evaluation warned that generic togetherness could return if specificity disappeared in production.

**Task 4 evidence.** Match cuts, reverse editing, top-down bounded surfaces and zip choreography created watchability. The evaluator still found the concepts one step from familiar product demonstration. The subtraction test proved execution independence in the narrow sense of surviving without polish, but not product ownership.

**Where the problem lies.** `SKILL` and `EVAL`. The Skill defines idea versus format/execution, but the evaluation does not force an execution-neutral proposition and product-causality test before rewarding watchability or subtraction.

**Smallest likely intervention.** Require every candidate to be written twice: one sentence with no medium, visual, edit, copy or asset; and one execution. Score the first sentence for leap and ownability before any production craft is considered. Clarify that subtraction of polish tests legibility, not specificity.

### 9. Critique timing

**Finding: critique is not generally killing ideas too early; the larger problem is that generation and critique are entangled, and critique sometimes repairs a weak premise through execution detail.**

**Task 2 evidence.** Independent critique was productive: it strengthened the role reversal and required reason → Piazza detail → response. It did not kill the route prematurely. However, the revision improved a portable platform without resolving whether distinctive real stories existed.

**Task 4 evidence.** Five routes were cut after each had a leap, truth, social mechanic, watch reason, three executions, genericity test and failure mode. Two survivors then received full development and two adversarial tests. `NO IDEA YET MEETS THE STANDARD` was appropriately timed and justified.

**Where the problem lies.** `AGENT ORCHESTRATION` secondarily, not the main `EVAL` failure. One path creates, anticipates objections and rejects in close succession. That encourages safe, evaluator-readable routes and may reduce incubation, but current evidence does not show promising ideas were killed before sufficient development.

**Smallest likely intervention.** Preserve the existing late independent verdict, but separate generation from adversarial evaluation. Give raw routes one short development pass before scoring, while applying only truth/safety gates immediately. Do not weaken the final fatal specificity test.

### 10. Generative convergence

**Finding: present, especially in Task 4. Names and devices vary more than the underlying human/action pattern.**

**Task 2 evidence.** Formal range was real, but several routes converged on “make lower-commitment gathering visible and prompt someone to meet/share.” The winner and runner-up both relied on recognisable social behaviour around bringing/sharing with another person. This is moderate convergence, not total duplication.

**Task 4 evidence.** Transformation, reversal, spatial metaphor, branching and chaptering all repeatedly communicated compact portability, activity variety and use during in-between moments. The evaluator explicitly distinguished seven devices from only modest proposition-level range.

**Where the problem lies.** `SKILL`, `PROMPT CONTRACT`, `AGENT ORCHESTRATION` and possibly `MODEL CAPABILITY`. Asking for a number of territories and different mechanisms rewards lexical/formal diversity. No process clusters outputs by premise, protagonist, action or payoff before claiming range.

**Smallest likely intervention.** After initial generation, anonymise and cluster the routes by (1) underlying promise, (2) protagonist, (3) action, (4) payoff and (5) temporal pattern. Count clusters, not names. Replace duplicates from deliberately different product truths or human roles. Use an independent divergent pass only if the first map remains concentrated.

## Root-cause synthesis

| Cause | Assessment | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| `SKILL` | **Primary** | The method requires relevant truth, different mechanisms and critique, but not causal product necessity, premise-level divergence, a forced creative transformation or an accumulating campaign engine. |
| `PROMPT CONTRACT` | **Primary** | Output volume and mechanism diversity can be satisfied without semantic diversity; ownability and product-substitution are not hard gates before development. |
| `AGENT ORCHESTRATION` | **Contributing** | The same reasoning stream diagnoses, generates, anticipates critique and prunes. Independent evaluation occurs after convergence rather than adding a distinct divergent search. |
| `EVAL` | **Mixed: strong detector, incomplete prevention** | Task 4’s fatal substitution test worked and Task 2’s review improved the route. Existing routine evals nevertheless gave high creative scores without stress-testing causal ownability, allowing the weakness to remain latent. |
| `KNOWLEDGE` | **Material in Task 4; insufficient as general explanation** | Missing construction, curation, refill and interaction truths blocked product-owned invention. Monzù had richer truth, yet Task 2 still relied on a portable mechanic. |
| `MODEL CAPABILITY` | **Plausible residual, not proven** | Repeated familiar abstractions across two business models suggest a ceiling, but current tests confound model, prompt, method, knowledge and orchestration. |

## Minimal remediation sequence to test later

This is a diagnostic recommendation, not an instruction to modify production Skills now.

1. Add a precondition: no claim of ownable invention without at least one consequential brand/product truth.
2. Make causal specificity and nearest-substitute failure pre-selection gates.
3. Measure divergence semantically across premise, human role, action and consequence—not by names or mechanism labels.
4. Insert one explicit creative-transformation pass between strategy and territory.
5. Separate platform-native craft from social consequence, and require an accumulating engine for campaign-platform requests.
6. Separate divergent generation from adversarial critique while retaining the final fatal gates.
7. Rerun Task 2 and Task 4 as controlled evaluations. Only after those interventions should remaining repeated failure be attributed more strongly to model capability.

The smallest useful change is therefore not “generate more ideas.” It is to change what qualifies as a different and ownable idea before the system spends effort developing executions.
