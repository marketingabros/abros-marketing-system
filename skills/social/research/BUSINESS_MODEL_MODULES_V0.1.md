# Business-Model Modules v0.1

Status: architecture definition only. These are module specifications, not final Skills.

Decision date: 2026-09-07

## Selection rule

A business-model module describes how demand, conversion and fulfilment work. It must be reusable by different industries and brands that share those mechanics.

Only three modules are justified by the currently known Abros portfolio:

1. Local Venue / Experience
2. Local Service
3. Ecommerce / Product

The Social Growth system should load one primary module per task. A secondary module is allowed only when a real customer journey spans two distinct commercial systems and the strategy preserves that distinction.

## 1. Local Venue / Experience

Current fit: restaurants, bars, kids activity venues and entertainment concepts where a customer must attend a place or time-bound experience.

### Business mechanics

- Demand is geographically constrained and often occasion-led.
- Value is realised through attendance, participation or an on-premise transaction.
- Capacity can expire by date, session, daypart or service period.
- Discovery may occur well before action, while action may occur through booking, enquiry, maps, phone, DM or walk-in.
- Empty capacity cannot be stored; excess demand may be commercially useless or harmful.
- Repeat behaviour depends on experience, new occasions, habit, programming or social connection.

### Relevant social objectives

- Qualified local awareness and consideration.
- Confidence about the experience, location, suitability and practical details.
- Demand for underfilled dates, sessions or dayparts.
- Launch or event sequencing without exhausting novelty.
- Appropriate action when capacity is available.
- Repeat attendance, advocacy and community connection.
- Demand protection when the operation is already constrained.

### Relevant customer behaviours

- Discovering through content, search, location tags, creators or peers.
- Evaluating occasion fit, distance, companions, timing, price cues and uncertainty.
- Checking current details and availability.
- Booking, joining a waitlist, requesting directions, calling, messaging or walking in.
- Attending, sharing proof and returning for another occasion.

### Conversion events

- Booking or reservation.
- Ticket/session purchase or registration.
- Qualified event or group enquiry.
- Waitlist join.
- Call, direction request or high-intent DM.
- Attributed or plausibly influenced visit/walk-in.
- Repeat visit or attendance.

Conversions must be configured per brand; no single event is assumed for all venues.

### Major constraints

- Catchment, travel time, opening hours and access.
- Capacity, availability, lead time, cancellations and no-shows.
- Time, weather, season, holidays and local events.
- Operational ability to fulfil the promise shown in content.
- Weak online-to-offline attribution and unattributed walk-ins.
- Privacy, consent and safety when guests or children appear.

### Useful metrics

- Locally relevant reach and discovery where measurable.
- Profile, location, map, call, link and qualified-message actions.
- Booking/registration pace by date, session, source and lead time.
- Attendance, utilisation, cancellations and repeat attendance.
- Capacity-adjusted conversion rather than total demand alone.
- Content response by occasion, journey stage and service window.
- Guest proof, referrals and repeat signals with consent.

### Common strategic errors

- Optimising broad reach when only local or time-relevant demand can convert.
- Promoting already constrained periods while weak periods remain underfilled.
- Treating total bookings as incremental social impact.
- Using the same objective and CTA before, during and after a launch.
- Publishing stale availability, hours or event information.
- Assuming attractive venue imagery resolves every customer uncertainty.
- Ignoring the distinction between interest, attendance and profitable repeat behaviour.

### Must remain outside this module

- Restaurant menus, dietary/allergy handling, table/service logic and food-specific proof.
- Kids safeguarding, age suitability and guardian-specific trust rules.
- Any brand's location, capacity, offer, audience or current availability.
- Instagram or other platform mechanics.
- Generic creative strategy, diagnosis and testing methods.

## 2. Local Service

Current fit: laundry businesses and future salons, repair, care or appointment/drop-off services that acquire and fulfil demand within a defined geography.

### Business mechanics

- Demand is constrained by service area, travel, collection/delivery radius or branch access.
- Conversion commonly begins with an enquiry, quote, appointment, order, drop-off or collection request.
- Trust, reliability, convenience and outcome confidence can matter more than entertainment reach.
- Capacity depends on staff, equipment, appointment slots, turnaround and logistics.
- Need may be recurring, urgent, seasonal or triggered by a specific problem.
- Repeat frequency and lifetime value can outweigh the first transaction.

### Relevant social objectives

- Local discovery among customers with a relevant need.
- Reduce uncertainty about process, quality, timing, care and convenience.
- Generate qualified enquiries, bookings or orders.
- Shift demand toward available branches, slots or services.
- Build trust through credible outcomes, people, process and proof.
- Encourage repeat use and habit formation.
- Support reputation recovery and service communication with human oversight.

### Relevant customer behaviours

- Searching after a need arises or noticing a solution before it becomes urgent.
- Comparing geography, turnaround, trust, price cues and convenience.
- Asking a qualifying question or requesting a quote.
- Booking, ordering, visiting, arranging collection/delivery or dropping off an item.
- Evaluating the delivered outcome and deciding whether to return or recommend.

### Conversion events

- Qualified call, form, DM or quote request.
- Appointment or service booking.
- Collection/delivery request.
- Online service order.
- Branch visit or drop-off where attribution is feasible.
- Completed service, repeat order and referral.

### Major constraints

- Service radius, branch coverage and customer travel friction.
- Staff, equipment, slot and logistics capacity.
- Turnaround promises and time-sensitive demand.
- Service variability, claims, complaints and reputation risk.
- Offline conversion and fragmented source attribution.
- Personal information in enquiries and service records.

### Useful metrics

- Relevant local reach, search/profile discovery and direction actions.
- Qualified enquiries by source, location and service need.
- Enquiry-to-booking/order and booking-to-completion rates.
- Cost or effort per qualified demand event where appropriate.
- Capacity/utilisation by branch, slot or service type.
- Turnaround, cancellation and repeat-use indicators.
- Reviews, referrals, complaint themes and response outcomes, handled with privacy controls.

### Common strategic errors

- Treating engagement or follower growth as demand quality.
- Using ecommerce purchase logic for a service that needs qualification or trust.
- Generating enquiries outside the service area or available capacity.
- Making universal speed, price or outcome promises.
- Posting generic tips that never reduce a real customer uncertainty.
- Ignoring completed service, repeat use and operational quality after lead generation.
- Automating sensitive service conversations.

### Must remain outside this module

- Laundry-specific material, garment-care claims or damage handling.
- Salon, repair or other industry-specific safety and professional rules.
- A brand's service list, geography, turnaround, prices or guarantees.
- Platform functionality and metric definitions.
- Cross-industry creative and diagnostic methods.

## 3. Ecommerce / Product

Current fit: My Treasure Bags and future brands where the principal conversion is an online product purchase followed by fulfilment.

### Business mechanics

- A customer can discover, evaluate and transact remotely.
- Product availability, margin, basket value, fulfilment and returns shape useful demand.
- Social may create discovery, reduce product uncertainty, support consideration, trigger purchase and stimulate repeat or referral.
- The journey can span platform content, profile, product page, cart, checkout and post-purchase experience.
- Product and creative fatigue, inventory and promotional dependence require active portfolio control.
- Attribution is incomplete across devices, dark social, organic discovery and delayed purchase.

### Relevant social objectives

- Qualified product/category discovery.
- Communicate use, differentiation, proof and fit.
- Reduce purchase friction and uncertainty.
- Support launches, stock moments and seasonal demand.
- Generate commercially sound product-page, cart and purchase activity.
- Increase repeat purchase, cross-sell, advocacy and UGC with consent.
- Learn which needs, products and creative mechanisms drive useful demand.

### Relevant customer behaviours

- Discovering a product through social, search, creators, advertising or peers.
- Comparing alternatives, use cases, trust, price, delivery and return risk.
- Viewing a product, saving/sharing, asking a question, adding to cart and purchasing.
- Receiving, using, reviewing, sharing and purchasing again.

### Conversion events

- Qualified product-page or collection-page visit.
- Product question or stock notification request.
- Add to cart and checkout initiation.
- Completed purchase with valid attribution caveats.
- Repeat purchase, cross-sell, referral or consented UGC.

### Major constraints

- Inventory, variant and fulfilment availability.
- Margin, discounting, delivery cost/time and return/refund conditions.
- Product truth, claims, age suitability, rights and safety.
- Tracking loss, view-through influence and multi-touch journeys.
- Creative saturation and over-reliance on promotional content.
- Differences between first purchase, gift purchase and repeat use.

### Useful metrics

- Qualified reach and product discovery by audience and content job.
- Product/profile/link actions with tagged destinations.
- Landing-page engagement and product-view quality where available.
- Add-to-cart, checkout and purchase rates with definitions preserved.
- Revenue, contribution or margin measures where appropriate and authorised.
- New versus repeat customers, basket value, returns and repeat interval.
- Creative performance by product, need, proof, format and audience—not only last-click revenue.

### Common strategic errors

- Treating every social post as a direct-response product ad.
- Optimising last-click revenue while ignoring discovery and delayed influence.
- Scaling demand for unavailable, low-margin or operationally constrained products.
- Assuming a high-view creative caused purchases without comparable evidence.
- Using discounts as the default conversion method.
- Mixing gift-giver, purchaser, user and parent/guardian audiences without distinction.
- Importing generic ecommerce benchmarks or funnels as brand truth.

### Must remain outside this module

- Kids/family safeguarding, guardian psychology or age-development expertise.
- Product-specific claims, prices, stock, audience and fulfilment promises.
- Platform ranking, formats and feature behaviour.
- General creative strategy, portfolio diagnosis and test design.
- Brand tone, visual identity and positioning.

## Modules intentionally not created

### Subscription / Membership

Not justified by a confirmed current Abros operating model. Create only when recurring access, retention, churn, renewal and member value are primary commercial mechanics.

### B2B / Lead Generation

Not justified by a confirmed current brand need. Create only when pipeline stages, account/role targeting, qualification, sales handoff and long-cycle attribution are required.

### Travel / Hotel

Not justified by a confirmed Abros hotel or travel business. It must never be inferred from restaurant “hospitality” research. A future module would require inventory by date, length of stay, destination demand, booking windows, distribution channels and travel-specific proof.

## Module governance

- Keep module principles brand-independent and platform-independent.
- Store current brand mechanics and constraints in Brand OS or runtime operational data, as appropriate.
- Treat module assumptions as hypotheses until tested across applicable brands.
- Record observations separately; require review before promoting them into a module.
- Add a module only after a real portfolio need and an admission review.
- Evaluate the core and each loaded module separately so a vertical cannot conceal weak general reasoning.
