# Content portfolio method

## 1. Establish the planning problem

Name the primary objective, audience behaviour, channel role, decision window, campaign stage and binding business constraint. Decide whether this is an always-on portfolio, a staged campaign or both.

## 2. Allocate content jobs

Choose only necessary jobs: discovery, orientation, demonstration, proof, objection reduction, action, reminder, participation, service, repeat or learning. Balance is contextual. A constrained venue may need demand shifting; a new product may need proof; a local service may need trust and process clarity.

## 3. Sequence the journey

Arrange items so later asks are earned by earlier understanding/proof. For a launch, distinguish anticipation, reveal, explanation, proof, action and sustainment. Repeat crucial information intentionally when audience exposure is uncertain, but change the entry point or proof rather than reposting cosmetic variants.

## 4. Check repetition and fatigue

Fingerprint recent content by:

- customer job and journey stage;
- subject/proof;
- core idea/territory;
- opening and narrative shape;
- visual device;
- format and CTA;
- offer/occasion/audience.

Repetition is justified when it strengthens memory, reaches a different audience or supports a new stage. It is risky when the same promise, device and audience exposure recur without new value. A performance decline alone does not prove fatigue.

## 5. Choose format last

Treat format as a delivery hypothesis. Select it because its sequence, motion, immediacy, interaction or persistence strengthens the job—not because a platform myth labels one format “best.” Load the relevant platform Skill for production details.

## 6. Allocate experiments

Reserve only experiments with a named uncertainty, one principal change, success signal and learning decision. Do not crowd out core demand/communication work to maximise novelty.

## 7. Feasibility and approval

Check assets, people, rights, operational truth, capacity/stock, production effort, accessibility and approval lead time. Mark dependencies and draft status.
